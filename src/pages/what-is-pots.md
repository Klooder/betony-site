# What is POTS?

Postural tachycardia syndrome (PoTS or POTS) is when the heart rate increases after getting up from sitting or lying down, often causing dizziness or light-headedness. It is also called postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome. There is currently no cure, but the NHS says it can be managed with lifestyle changes or medicines.

## Symptoms
POTS affects everyone differently. Some symptoms happen on standing and can ease when sitting or lying down:
- dizziness or light-headedness
- noticeable heartbeats (palpitations)
- chest pain and shortness of breath
- shaking and sweating
- fainting or nearly fainting

Other possible symptoms include stomach and digestion problems, headaches, blurred or tunnel vision, hands and feet turning purple, extreme tiredness, sleeping badly, and problems with thinking and memory, often called brain fog. Symptoms may be worse in the morning and can change from day to day.

## What can make symptoms worse
The cause of POTS is not clear. It can develop suddenly or gradually, and it is more likely in people with long COVID, ME/CFS or joint hypermobility syndrome. Things the NHS lists as making symptoms worse:
- getting too hot
- standing or sitting upright for a long time
- eating, especially carbohydrates
- not drinking enough fluids
- alcohol
- resting too much, or too much exercise
- being on your period

## How POTS is diagnosed
A GP may check blood pressure and heart rate before and after standing up; repeating this several times is called an active stand test. Blood tests and an ECG can check for other causes. A specialist may use further tests, including 24-hour heart rate and blood pressure monitoring or a tilt table test. Because the symptoms resemble other conditions, diagnosis can take time.

## Treatment
Finding the right treatment can take time. According to the NHS, a doctor may suggest lifestyle changes first and may prescribe medicines; several may need to be tried. Treatment decisions belong with a doctor who knows your history.
