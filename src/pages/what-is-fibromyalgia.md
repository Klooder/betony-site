# What is fibromyalgia?

Fibromyalgia, also called fibromyalgia syndrome (FMS), is a long-term condition that causes pain all over the body. The NHS says there is currently no cure, but treatment can ease some of the symptoms.

## Symptoms
Besides widespread pain, symptoms include:
- increased sensitivity to pain
- muscle stiffness
- difficulty getting to sleep or staying asleep, leading to fatigue
- problems with concentration and memory, known as "fibro fog"
- headaches
- irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
- frustration, worry or low mood

The symptoms are changeable: they can suddenly improve or get worse.

## Causes
The exact cause is unknown. It is thought to involve changes in the way the central nervous system processes pain messages. Some people may be more likely to develop it because of inherited genes, and it often appears to be triggered by something physical or emotional, such as an injury, an infection or stress.

## Who gets fibromyalgia
Anyone can develop fibromyalgia, but it is more common in women than men. It typically develops between the ages of 25 and 55. Some estimates suggest nearly 1 in 20 people may be affected to some degree. The true number is unclear, partly because there is no specific test and the symptoms resemble other conditions.

## Treatment
According to the NHS, treatment tends to combine lifestyle changes, talking therapies and medicine. What fits one person is decided with their doctor.
