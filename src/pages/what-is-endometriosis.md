# What is endometriosis?

Endometriosis is a chronic disease in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus, causing inflammation and scar tissue. It most often occurs in the pelvis, but it can also be found elsewhere, including the abdomen and chest. According to the WHO, it affects an estimated 10% of women of reproductive age worldwide, about 190 million people. It can affect anyone who has or had a womb, from their first period through menopause.

## Symptoms
Symptoms vary widely, and some people have none. Common ones listed by the WHO and NHS:
- severe period pain that stops normal activities
- heavy periods
- pain in the lower tummy and back (pelvic area)
- pain when going to the toilet
- pain during or after sex
- extreme tiredness
- bloating and nausea
- difficulty getting pregnant

Endometriosis can also affect mental health, including low mood and anxiety.

## Why diagnosis takes so long
The WHO puts the average time to diagnosis at between 4 and 12 years. Symptoms overlap with other conditions such as adenomyosis, fibroids, pelvic inflammatory disease and irritable bowel syndrome. Diagnosis can involve an ultrasound or MRI scan, and a laparoscopy, a small camera passed through a cut in the tummy, can confirm it. The WHO notes that a careful menstrual history, including pain and how heavy bleeding is, can help with diagnosis, and the NHS advises giving your GP as much information about your symptoms as you can.

## Causes and treatment
The cause of endometriosis is unknown, and there is currently no cure. Treatment aims to control symptoms and can include pain relief, hormonal medicines and surgery. The choice depends on the person, including whether they want to become pregnant, and is made with a doctor.
