# What is ME/CFS?

Myalgic encephalomyelitis, also called chronic fatigue syndrome or ME/CFS, is a long-term condition that can affect different parts of the body. Its cause is unknown, and it can affect anyone, including children.

## The four main symptoms
According to the NHS, the four main symptoms are:
- **Fatigue:** feeling extremely tired all the time, which can make daily activities like showering, work or school difficult.
- **Sleep problems:** insomnia, sleeping too much, or waking up feeling unrefreshed, exhausted or stiff.
- **Brain fog:** problems with thinking, concentration and memory.
- **Post-exertional malaise (PEM):** symptoms getting worse after physical or mental activity, possibly taking weeks to recover.

Some people also have pain in different parts of the body or flu-like symptoms such as a high temperature, headache and aching joints or muscles.

## What PEM means
PEM is what sets ME/CFS apart from ordinary tiredness. Activity, physical or mental, is followed by a worsening of symptoms, and recovery can take weeks.

## How ME/CFS is diagnosed
There is no specific test. ME/CFS is diagnosed from the symptoms and by ruling out other conditions, often with blood and urine tests. In the UK, NICE guideline NG206 (2021) covers diagnosing and managing ME/CFS and aims to help people get diagnosed earlier.

## Treatment
There is currently no cure. The NHS lists energy management, cognitive behavioural therapy and medicine for symptoms such as pain and sleep problems among the treatments that may help. Symptoms can get better or worse over time.
